# App Publishing Guide

## Developer Account Costs

### 1. Google Play Store (Android)
* **Cost:** $25 (One-time fee)
* **Details:** You only pay this once. Once you have a Google Play Developer account, you can publish as many apps as you want, and there are no yearly renewal fees.

### 2. Apple App Store (iOS)
* **Cost:** $99 / year (Recurring annual fee)
* **Details:** Apple's developer program requires a yearly subscription. You must keep paying this every year; if your subscription expires, your app will be temporarily removed from the App Store until you renew it.

**Total Upfront Cost:** $124 to get started on both platforms. 

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Do I pay for each app?**
> **NO.** You do not pay per app. You pay **per developer account**. Once you pay the $25 to Google and the $99/year to Apple, you can publish **unlimited apps** on both platforms for free.

*(Note: When you are ready to set these up, you will just need a standard credit card and a Google/Apple ID)*

## Publishing for Multiple Clients (White-Label Apps)

Since you have 4-5 different clients who need their own apps, you have two ways to handle the developer accounts:

### Strategy 1: The Client Model (Highly Recommended for Apple)
* **How it works:** Each of your 5 clients creates and pays for their *own* developer accounts ($99/yr for Apple, $25 for Google) under their own business name. They then invite you as a "Developer" or "Admin" to their account.
* **Why this is best:** Apple has a very strict rule (Guideline 4.2.6) stating that apps created from a commercialized template or app generation service MUST be submitted directly by the provider of the app's content (your client), not the developer (you). If you try to publish 5 similar grocery apps under your single account, Apple will likely reject them as "Spam".

### Strategy 2: The Agency Model (Better for Google)
* **How it works:** You pay the fees once, and you publish all 5 apps under your single Developer Account (e.g., "Your Company Name").
* **Why this is best:** It's much cheaper and less hassle for the clients. Google Play is generally okay with this as long as the apps look distinct. However, this is very risky for Apple, and they will likely force you to switch to Strategy 1 eventually.

## What Problems Will We Face? (App Review Roadblocks)

When you submit an app to Apple or Google, a human reviewer tests it before it goes live. Because you are publishing 4-5 apps that use the same underlying code (just with different logos and colors), here are the exact roadblocks you might face:

### 🔴 Apple App Store Problems
1. **The "Spam" Rule (Guideline 4.3):** Apple strongly dislikes it when a developer uploads 5 apps that do the exact same thing (e.g., 5 grocery delivery apps). If they look too similar, the reviewer will reject apps 2 through 5 and tell you to combine them into one single app where users pick their store from a list.
2. **The "Template" Rule (Guideline 4.2.6):** Apple explicitly states that apps created from a "white-label" template *must* be submitted by the client who owns the store, not the web developer who built it. If they catch you uploading a client's app on your account, they will reject it until the client makes their own $99 Apple account.

### 🟢 Google Play Store Problems
1. **The "Repetitive Content" Policy:** Google is much more relaxed than Apple, but if you upload 5 identical apps on the same day, their automated bots might flag your account for spamming. (Solution: Upload them a few days apart, and make sure their app icons and screenshots look different).
2. **The "20 Tester" Rule (For New Accounts):** If you create a brand new *Personal* developer account today, Google requires you to find 20 real people to install and test your app for 14 straight days before you are allowed to publish it. *(Tip: You can completely bypass this annoying rule if you register as an official Business account instead of a Personal account!)*
