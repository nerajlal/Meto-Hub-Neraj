# MetoHub: complete marketing and growth plan

Prepared: 3 November 2026. Market: India, starting with Kerala. Website: https://metohub.cloud.

This is an operating plan for acquiring, activating and retaining merchants. It is not a forecast of guaranteed sales. Budgets, targets and conversion assumptions below are proposed planning inputs, not historical MetoHub results or market benchmarks. No campaigns, messages, discounts or affiliate payments are authorized or launched by this document.

## 1. The business objective

Help small product sellers turn an Instagram audience into an organized online storefront, then earn recurring subscription revenue by making that store useful every week.

The primary outcome is **retained paying stores**, supported by activated trial stores. Followers, views, cheap leads and registrations are diagnostic metrics, not the final objective.

Recommended sequence:

1. Find one narrow seller segment with a repeated problem.
2. Help a small group launch real catalogs.
3. Learn why they activate, pay, renew or leave.
4. Turn successful onboarding into a repeatable process.
5. Scale the channels that produce retained merchants at an affordable cost.

Initial positioning: **A polished storefront for Instagram sellers, from ₹99/month.**

Core promise: **Your products, sizes and orders in one store link—without building a website from scratch.**

## 3. Audience and first beachhead

Start with **Kerala-based Instagram clothing and accessories sellers already receiving orders**, especially those comfortable offering COD. This recommendation is a hypothesis to validate in interviews, not a claim that this segment is proven best.

| Segment | Main problem | Demonstration | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clothing/boutiques | Repeated size/price questions and scattered order details | Catalog → size option → COD order → merchant dashboard | First |
| Jewelry/accessories | Presentation and product discovery | Gallery, collections, branding and shareable store link | First |
| Perfume/beauty | Explaining product options and organizing selections | Product information, volume variants and collections | Second |
| Home décor/handmade | Selling a coherent collection | Brand story, images and theme customization | Second |
| Watches/gadgets | Product details, options and inventory | Catalog detail, stock and tracking | Later test |
| Grocery/local essentials | Repeat ordering and operational fit | Compact catalog and reorder flow | Separate campaign |

An ideal early prospect has an active product catalog, existing customer inquiries, usable product photos, a clear owner, willingness to set up a store and a fulfillment process compatible with current checkout.

Lower-fit prospects include service-only businesses, businesses requiring prepaid-only checkout today, advanced warehouse/ERP requirements, or sellers expecting MetoHub to deliver sales without doing their own marketing. Qualify these openly instead of buying unsuitable signups.

### Discovery interview: 15 minutes

Ask about the last real order, how price/size availability was explained, where the customer address was recorded, how inventory was updated, how many tools were used, what went wrong, current website spend, and whether COD works for them. Ask to see the workflow with private customer information hidden.

Do not begin by pitching themes. End by asking: “Would a store link solve this enough that you would use it this week?” Record actual behavior and objections. Interview at least ten relevant sellers before treating the positioning as validated.

## 4. Positioning and message architecture

Lead with a recognizable workflow problem, prove the solution on screen and make the next action obvious.

**Main headline:** Out of the DMs. Into your own storefront.

**Supporting copy:** Give customers one place to browse your collection, choose an available option and place a cash-on-delivery order. Customize your store with your brand and start from ₹99/month after your free trial.

**Primary CTA:** Create my store.

**Secondary CTA:** Explore demo stores.

**Trial caption:** 30-day free trial. No credit card required to start, subject to confirming the current signup flow.

| Audience | Opening hook | Proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Boutique owner | Still answering “price?” and “available size?” one chat at a time? | Show a real product with options and an order |
| Jewelry seller | Your collection deserves more than a long chat thread. | Show collections and branded gallery |
| New micro-brand | Give your first collection its own store link. | Show setup from logo to public storefront |
| Existing seller | Keep Instagram for discovery. Give orders their own place. | Show link in bio → store → order management |

Never promise the store eliminates customer conversations. It reduces repetitive catalog work while letting the seller keep their own customer relationship.

### Language strategy

Use English for the website and documentation, and test Malayalam-speaking demos with English subtitles for Kerala acquisition. Start with a Malayalam hook such as “Instagram-il products sell cheyyunnundo?” only after a fluent speaker reviews the final recording. Use plain language; translate naturally rather than word for word. Expand into another language only when onboarding/support can serve those merchants.

## 5. Offers and packaging

Keep the price ladder simple: Base is the accessible start; Pro is for a branded domain and growing merchant needs. Highlight the reason to upgrade rather than suggesting Base is inadequate.

Offer a self-serve trial to everyone. During the initial learning phase, offer a small number of **assisted launch slots** with an explicit scope: theme selection, logo/colors, up to five products and a launch checklist. Do not promise unlimited catalog setup for ₹99/month. Clearly disclose whether assistance is free, limited, or charged separately; no setup fee is assumed in this plan.

Avoid permanent “50% off” campaigns and resetting countdown timers. Use genuine workshop dates or real assistance capacity when urgency is useful. Do not add referral credits, coupons, free extra months or special prices to public copy until the business approves the terms and the product can honor them.

## 6. Acquisition funnel and merchant activation

```text
Relevant seller sees content / receives an introduction
→ visits a segment-specific demo
→ starts signup
→ verifies email
→ adds brand details and products
→ previews a usable store
→ publishes and shares the link
→ receives a genuine customer order
→ chooses a paid plan
→ renews and continues operating
```

Proposed activation definition: verified account + brand identity + at least three active products with prices, usable images and stock/options + at least one collection + successfully tested storefront checkout + store link published/shared. A test order helps validate setup but is not a genuine customer sale.

Track time to first product, time to activation, time to link sharing and time to first genuine order separately. A paid merchant with an unused catalog is an early retention risk.

### Landing-page priorities

- Explain who it is for and show the current checkout limitation near the decision point.
- Present ₹99 entry pricing with the real trial length and clear annual billing terms.
- Show the customer storefront and merchant order dashboard, not only theme thumbnails.
- Link to `/themes`, `/pricing`, `/features`, `/blogs`, contact and policies.
- Give each major segment a relevant demo; do not send every ad to the same generic section.
- Use real screenshots without customer addresses, emails, phone numbers or payment details.
- Add genuine merchant evidence only with permission and verifiable context.

Do not build many thin landing pages initially. Start with one strong page and clearly labeled fashion/jewelry demos; add dedicated pages when segment-level conversion evidence justifies them.

## 8. Founder-led outreach playbook

Build a small prospect list manually from relevant public business information and introductions. Suggested CRM fields: store name, segment, public profile, contact source, consent/contact status, current workflow, main objection, next action/date, signup source, activation date, plan and renewal outcome.

Daily starting capacity: research five relevant businesses and send up to five personalized introductions where outreach is welcome. This is a workload limit, not a sales target. Avoid scraping personal contacts or bulk unsolicited messaging.

**First message:**

> Hi [name], I’m [founder] from MetoHub in Kochi. I noticed your [specific collection]. We’re building a simple store link for Instagram sellers so customers can browse products and place COD orders. Would you like a short demo using a sample boutique?

**After permission:**

> Here’s the demo: [tracked link]. Plans start at ₹99/month after the 30-day trial. Customer checkout currently supports COD. If that fits your business, I can help you set up the first few products.

**One respectful follow-up after several days:**

> Hi [name], checking whether the demo was useful. Was the main concern setup, payments, pricing or something else? No problem if it isn’t relevant right now.

Stop after a decline or silence following the agreed follow-up. Record objections. Never claim to have built their store or use their logo/images publicly without approval.

## 10. WhatsApp and assisted selling

Use a public business number and a clear identity. Keep initial conversations permission-based. Do not import the merchant's customers into marketing lists.

Qualify with three questions: What do you sell? Do you already take orders? Does COD fit your fulfillment? Offer a short demo, then a setup link. Ask whether follow-up help is wanted and record preferences.

Suggested labels: New inquiry, Qualified, Demo sent, Trial started, Setup needed, Activated, Paid, Not suitable and Do not contact. These can be manual WhatsApp/CRM labels; no automated CRM feature is claimed.

Proposed reply target: within four business hours during the actual published support schedule. This is an internal target, not a 24/7 promise. Use an honest away message.

Click-to-WhatsApp ads can open a business conversation from Facebook/Instagram; treat an actual qualified conversation as the lead, not just the button click. See the [official WhatsApp guide](https://whatsappbusiness.com/resources/resource-library/ads-that-click-to-whatsapp-smb-guide/).

## 11. Partnerships, creators and referrals

Pilot with two or three relevant partners: boutique photographers, freelance designers, local seller trainers, packaging businesses or small business communities. Favor access to real merchants over a large unrelated audience.

Partner kit: one-page product explanation, approved claims, real demo links, price/trial terms, COD limitation, contact person, tracked link and approved creatives. Require customer permission for any portfolio example.

A referral arrangement is a proposal, not an existing product feature. If approved, begin with a manually maintained referral ledger and written terms. Pay rewards only after a real paid invoice and refund window; cap amounts using contribution margin, exclude self-referrals and duplicate identities, and define reversals. Do not promise revenue share without operational and accounting support.

For creators, agree deliverables, audience fit, usage rights, disclosure and dates before payment. Paid/material relationships need clear advertising disclosure under the [ASCI influencer guidance](https://www.ascionline.in/social/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ASCI-Influencer-Guidelines.pdf). Never buy fabricated merchant reviews.

## 13. Paid media: gated experiments

Do not spend heavily until verified email, product setup, checkout, notifications and billing have been tested on the live environment. Check platform tracking separately from merchant-store tracking: a merchant's GA4/Meta setup does not automatically instrument MetoHub's own acquisition funnel.

First experiment: one segment, one geography/language hypothesis, two or three creative messages and one destination. Avoid splitting a small budget into many audiences. Test the actual options available in Ads Manager rather than relying on a permanent list of interests/objectives.

Candidate messages: repetitive catalog questions, brand presentation and organized orders. Use real product recordings. Include the COD limitation and trial/price terms in the destination. Choose the destination deliberately: website for self-serve users, opt-in WhatsApp for sellers needing help.

**Proposed test controls:** define maximum spend before launch; observe at least a full weekly cycle where feasible; stop immediately for broken links, tracking, signup, mail or policy issues. If a creative spends twice the pre-agreed target qualified-lead cost without a qualified lead, review it; this is a test rule, not a statistical conclusion. Pause channel scaling when paid CAC exceeds the business's contribution-margin budget. Do not make claims from tiny samples.

Retarget only with appropriate consent/audience controls and sufficient volume. Exclude already converted merchants where possible; do not advertise a trial repeatedly to paying customers. Do not introduce server-side event forwarding without a separate implementation and privacy review.

Search ads are a second-stage test for explicit storefront intent. Review actual search terms and exclude unrelated jobs, courses, free-code requests and enterprise features the product does not serve. Broad traffic campaigns are not the starting strategy for a ₹99 subscription.

## 15. Unit economics and budget limits

Calculate economics before approving acquisition spend.

- **Cash CAC:** attributable acquisition spend / new paying merchants from that cohort.
- **Fully loaded CAC:** acquisition spend + attributable labor/tools/rewards / those new paying merchants.
- **Contribution per merchant-month:** recognized subscription revenue less taxes collected, payment fees, variable infrastructure, variable support and attributable rewards/refunds.
- **Payback months:** CAC / positive monthly contribution.
- **Trial-to-paid:** paid merchants from a fully matured trial cohort / eligible trials in that cohort.
- **Activation rate:** activated stores / verified trials in the same cohort.
- **Monthly logo churn:** paying stores lost during month / paying stores at the start.
- **MRR:** normalized recurring paid subscription revenue; annual plans divided by 12, excluding tax, one-off work and unpaid trials.

Do not estimate lifetime value from a handful of merchants. A revenue/churn shortcut can mislead before retention is stable.

### Illustrative acquisition ceiling—not actual margin

Assume a 70% contribution margin only for a first sensitivity calculation and aim for three-month payback:

| Monthly plan | Illustrative contribution/month | Three-month acquisition ceiling |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Base ₹99 | ₹69.30 | ₹207.90 |
| Pro ₹249 | ₹174.30 | ₹522.90 |

A paid ad producing a ₹1,000 CAC is difficult to justify for a Base merchant at the illustrated contribution. Founder-led acquisition is not free either: record time spent.

## 17. Retention and customer success

Every week, review three groups: verified but not activated, activated without genuine orders and paying merchants with little recent use. Contact only through permitted channels and offer a specific useful action.

Reasons to stay: catalog is current, brand looks right, orders are manageable, notifications are useful and pricing is understandable. Themes alone are not a retention strategy.

Create short help guides for adding products/options, changing branding, sharing a link, managing COD orders, entering tracking, invoices, custom domains, billing/cancellation and exporting data. Do not promise features in a guide before testing them.

Review cancellations monthly using categories such as payment mismatch, no customer traffic, setup complexity, missing capability, business paused, support experience and price/value. Separate product-fit problems from marketing claims that attracted the wrong merchants.

Ask active, consenting merchants for feedback after they have actually used the product. Record quote, permission, date, store link, what was used and any incentive. For a case study, show baseline, timeframe and evidence; distinguish correlation from a claim that MetoHub caused sales growth.

### Illustrative operating milestones

A learning target might be 10 interviews, 10–20 assisted trial stores, five permissioned usability sessions, two partner pilots and three verified case-study candidates over 90 days. These are proposed workload goals, not expected paid-customer results. Set conversion targets after measuring the first complete cohort.

## 20. Weekly operating rhythm and responsibilities

Founder owns positioning, interviews, qualification and weekly channel decisions. Content owner produces demos and publishes approved claims. Product/engineering owns funnel reliability and instrumentation. Support owns permission-based assistance and recurring objections. Finance/operations owns paid revenue reconciliation, refunds and acquisition-cost calculation. One person may initially hold multiple roles, but each task needs an owner.

- **Monday:** review cohort scorecard, critical issues and this week's single funnel priority.
- **Tuesday/Wednesday:** publish demos, interview sellers and run agreed setup sessions.
- **Thursday:** review partner/referral experiment and publish one useful guide.
- **Friday:** reconcile spend and paid accounts, record learnings and decide what to stop.
- **Daily:** check signup/mail failures, inbound questions and unresolved onboarding blockers.

Maintain one experiment log: hypothesis, segment, offer, creative, destination, budget, dates, success rule, observed results, sample size, confounders and decision. Change one main variable at a time when feasible.

Start with a spreadsheet and existing tools. Buy additional CRM/email/video subscriptions only when manual operation becomes a measured bottleneck. No particular paid tool purchase is required by this plan.

## 23. Risks, response and stop conditions

| Risk | Response |
| --- | --- |
| Ad claim attracts prepaid-only merchants | Make COD visible and qualify earlier |
| Many trial registrations, few usable stores | Improve product setup before increasing traffic |
| Cheap subscriptions require expensive manual support | Bound assistance; improve self-serve guidance; measure contribution |
| Too many themes overwhelm new sellers | Recommend three relevant themes while keeping the full gallery available |
| Trial duration conflicts across pages | Audit live website, emails, signup and policies against configuration |
| Fictional reviews mistaken for real proof | Preserve labels and replace with permissioned evidence when available |
| Mail/checkout/billing failures | Pause affected campaigns, fix and revalidate before resuming |
| Partner sends low-fit or fake signups | Pay only against verified eligible paid outcomes; investigate anomalies |
| High acquisition cost or early churn | Hold spend and interview affected cohorts |
| Personal data appears in marketing/screenshots | Remove/redact; review asset workflow before publishing again |

A marketing plan does not justify changing product capabilities or live billing terms. Changes to discounts, pricing, trial duration, tracking and automation need their own implementation/approval process.
